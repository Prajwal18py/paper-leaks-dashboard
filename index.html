Patterns

State recruitment exams are the epicentre. ~80% of all incidents are state-conducted (public service commissions, subordinate boards, state police) — CBSE recurs most, alongside repeat cases in UP, Rajasthan and Bihar. National exams (NEET, SSC, CBSE) get the headlines, but state-level exams are the bulk. Because most exams are run by state governments of many different parties, these counts shouldn't be read as a verdict on any single government.

Response has hardened over time. Exams cancelled in response to a leak rose from ~17% of incidents in 2004–2014 to ~62% in 2014–2026 — institutions increasingly choose to scrap the paper rather than risk a compromised result standing.

Convictions are rare and slow. The recurring pattern is arrests and CBI/SIT probes, with convictions — where they come at all — arriving years later. Only 10 of 63 incidents with reported arrests also report a conviction outcome.

Peak years

Incidents cluster hard in 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024 (14, 15, 11, 12 incidents respectively) — the window in which paper leaks became a front-line political and street-protest issue in India, driven by cases like NEET-UG, UKSSSC, RRB-NTPC and various state SSC exams.

⚠ Big caveat — read before comparing periods

The raw counts for the two periods are shaped by a real reporting & digitisation bias, and several forces all push the same way — fewer early incidents recorded, not necessarily fewer occurring:

  1. Digitisation & the smartphone. Pre-2012 news is sparsely archived online, and WhatsApp — which both causes modern leaks and documents them virally — barely existed in the earlier period.
  2. Missingness is heavy on outcome fields. arrests 43% missing, convictions 91% missing, aspirants_affected 60% missing, linked_deaths 96% missing. Treat these as "not reported," not zero.
  3. Unit inconsistency. aspirants_affected mixes registered-candidate counts with retest-only counts across rows — sum with caution.

Read the patterns above as the trustworthy signal; weight rows by their confidence and leak_status fields when aggregating further. This is a record of reported incidents, not a complete census.

Methodology & terms

What the fields on this file mean

Confirmed — the leak was acknowledged by the conducting body, investigating agency, or court, or is backed by multiple independent reports.
Alleged — credibly reported by press or aspirants, but not yet acknowledged by an official body.
Denied — the conducting body has publicly denied a leak took place, despite reports.
Suspected — pattern (e.g. abnormal scores, viral content pre-exam) suggests a leak, without confirmation either way.
Confidence — how solid the sourcing is: High (multiple reputable outlets / official statement), Medium (single reputable source), Low (single or unverified source).
Arrests vs. convictions — an arrest means someone was detained in connection with the case; a conviction means a court found someone guilty. Most cases here show the former without the latter, often because the case is still pending, not because no one was guilty.

Bottom line

  • Incidents are up 3.6× since 2014, largely concentrated in state-run recruitment exams in Madhya Pradesh and its neighbors — but treat the rise with caution: better digital reporting and RTI coverage in the later period inflate the raw comparison.
  • Punishment lags detection badly. 1,692 people have been arrested across 63 cases, but only 114 convictions are on record — roughly 1 in 6 arrest-cases shows a completed conviction in this dataset.
  • Institutions increasingly cancel rather than retest — 57 exams were scrapped outright vs 23 retests, suggesting a preference for a clean restart over patching a compromised paper.
